Design round 2: game views

The landing page is decided: Forge from round 1. Round 1's game screens all read as generated, because they shared one skeleton (centred title, divider, drop cap, text, two buttons, chips, round portrait, three stat tiles) and only changed the skin. These four change the composition instead, borrow the vocabulary of real games, and stay in the Forge family (forged metal, ember, runes) with warmer bronze, copper and blood instead of grey. Each opens on the game screen; "Back to map" and the locations work, and energy goes down as you play.

The landscapes are placeholder art drawn in SVG. Real illustrations in the style of the landing logo would lift every one of these more than any CSS can.

A. Panorama

Open
  • Idea: every location is a painted scene; the story is a caption under it, as in Darkest Dungeon.
  • Navigation: a leather rail of bronze icon slots; the active slot glows like an ember.
  • Energy: ten flames in the top bar. Character: portrait in an arch over a scene, stats as pips, traits as wax seals.
  • Map: postcards, each with its own little landscape.

The most atmospheric and the best fit for a text-led game: the scene sets the mood, the text stays left-aligned and readable. It depends most on art; with real illustrations it becomes the strongest.

B. Map table

Open
  • Idea: the map is the game, drawn on a table by candlelight; locations are wax-sealed pins, the outcome arrives as a letter laid over the map's edge.
  • Navigation: brass drawer plates; the lit one is the current view. Energy: a burning candle wick.
  • Character: a brass medallion with a name banner and a small ledger.

The most original and the clearest loop (map → seal → letter → map), and it grows naturally as locations unlock and the fog lifts. The map needs care on a phone, where it becomes a pannable image or falls back to a list.

C. Fate cards

Open
  • Idea: an adventure is a card drawn from the location's deck, so chance is visible; the card turns over as you draw.
  • Navigation: small card backs; the active one is lifted out of the hand. Energy: ten ember gems.
  • Map: a row of decks; locked decks are bound in chains and sealed.

The strongest game metaphor for random outcomes, and later choices fit it well (play a card from your hand). The risk is tone: cards on velvet lean towards a gambling table more than a muddy road.

D. Reliquary

Open
  • Idea: action-RPG vocabulary: energy is an orb of liquid fire, rewards are item slots with rarity borders and tooltips, the character has an equipment doll.
  • Navigation: spiked plaques with diamond sockets. Gothic spiked corners on every frame.

Reads most like "a real game" at first glance, and it is ready for inventory and equipment (v0.2, v0.4). It is also the most familiar look, closest to what players have seen in many ARPGs, and the text becomes secondary to the chrome.

Recommendation

  • Panorama as the base for the game screen, with the Map table as the map view: the scene and caption for the outcome, the drawn map with seals for choosing where to go. Together they cover the whole v0.2 loop and give each screen its own memorable thing.
  • Worth taking from the others: Reliquary's item slots with rarity borders for rewards and inventory, and its energy orb if the flames feel too small.
  • For round 3: real art. If you can generate illustrations the way you made the logo (the Clearing, the Old Forest, the Mill, a character portrait, in the landing logo's painterly style), the scenes replace the SVG silhouettes.
