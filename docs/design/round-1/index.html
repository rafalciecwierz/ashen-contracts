Design round 1: sketches

Five directions from the brief in docs/DESIGN.md. Each sketch is a landing page and the desktop game screen. "Log in" opens the game with the outcome of an adventure in the Old Forest; "Back to map", the locations and "Go again" work, and energy goes down as you play. Click a screenshot to open the sketch. Mobile, the component sheet and refinement come in round 2, for the one or two directions picked here.

1. Crypt

Open sketch

Reference: Darkest Dungeon.

Type: Grenze Gotisch, Alegreya.

Palette: stone grey, inked black, bone, blood red; torchlight only as lighting.

Memorable thing: heavy inked iron plates with rivets.

Strong mood and cheap to build (CSS only). The weak spot is distinctiveness: stone, blood and a gothic face are what most dark-fantasy UIs reach for, and the brick wall tiles visibly.

2. Grimoire

Open sketch

Reference: a Dungeons & Dragons tome.

Type: Texturina, Vollkorn.

Palette: oxblood leather, vellum, iron-gall ink, oak-gall green, gilt.

Memorable thing: the game inside an open book, ribbon bookmarks as navigation, illuminated initials.

The most legible for long reading (dark ink on vellum) and the clearest metaphor. The book breaks on a phone: two pages and side ribbons have to become something else, which costs design work in round 2.

3. Broadside

Open sketch

Reference: the Witcher's Northern Kingdoms, woodcut broadsides.

Type: New Rocker, Sorts Mill Goudy.

Palette: ash-grey rag paper, woodblock black, cinnabar, wolf silver, dark wood.

Memorable thing: every surface is a printed notice nailed to wood, framed in cut-wood hatching and a folk band; ash falls on the landing page.

The most distinctive and the closest to "contracts for coin". Two-colour print gives strong hierarchy with few tokens, and offset shadows make buttons feel physical. It is a light theme, which few dark-fantasy games dare, so it needs a deliberate dark counterpart later (night, or the stronghold).

4. Forge

Open sketch

Reference: the owner's landing logo.

Type: Metamorphous, EB Garamond.

Palette: cold slate, forged iron, ash white, ember glow.

Memorable thing: bevelled iron frames and a glowing rune ring over the active panel; the logo itself with rising embers on the landing page.

The landing page is the most spectacular, because the logo does the work. The game screens look like many mobile RPGs: dark plus orange glow is exactly the generated-design tell the skill warns about, and the iron gradients are the hardest to turn into tokens.

5. Lantern

Open sketch

Reference: Fallen London, Sunless Sea.

Type: Bodoni Moda, Newsreader.

Palette: abyssal teal, verdigris, brass, bone, lamplight.

Memorable thing: storylet cards with brass filigree and oval cameos; a lighthouse beam sweeps the landing page.

Elegant and calm, with the best map (cards with cameos). It is the furthest from the Witcher and Darkest Dungeon: Victorian sea-gothic rather than a dirty road, so it fits the references only in part.

Recommendation

  • Take Broadside to round 2 as the main direction: it scores highest on mood and distinctiveness, and its inked, two-colour look also fits the outlined style of logo-game.svg (in a new palette, without the violet).
  • Take Grimoire as the second, if you want a darker, richer alternative to compare against in full, mobile included.
  • Keep Forge's landing idea whatever wins: the logo with embers is the strongest first impression. Round 2 can test it on top of the chosen direction.
  • Ideas worth borrowing across directions: Lantern's map cards with cameos, Crypt's rivets for locked or sealed things, Grimoire's illuminated initial.
